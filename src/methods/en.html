Body surface area

Du Bois and Du Bois: BSA = 0.007184 × weight^0.425 × height^0.725 (kg, cm).

Reference sets

Devereux RB et al. 2012 (Am J Cardiol 110:1189–94; 1,207 people aged 15 and over): sinuses of Valsalva expected (cm) = 2.423 + 0.009 × age + 0.461 × BSA − 0.267 × sex (1 male, 2 female), SEE 0.261 cm.

Lang RM et al. 2015 (ASE/EACVI chamber quantification), sex-specific mean ± SD. Men: annulus 26 ± 3, sinuses 34 ± 3, junction 29 ± 3, proximal ascending 30 ± 4 mm. Women: 23 ± 2, 30 ± 3, 26 ± 3, 27 ± 4 mm. Not adjusted for age or body size.

WASE 2022 (Asch FM et al., J Am Soc Echocardiogr 35:267–74): BSA-indexed mean ± SD by sex and age group (18–40, 41–65, over 65) for the annulus, sinuses and junction, multiplied by BSA. The values come from two independent transcriptions of the paper’s tables that agree with each other; the paper itself couldn’t be opened. WASE doesn’t cover the ascending aorta, so that level uses Lang 2015.

Campens et al. 2014 published age-, sex- and BSA-adjusted equations at all levels, but their coefficients couldn’t be verified, so they aren’t offered.

Uncertainty and conventions

The normal range is expected ± 2 SD. The range after each z-score shows the z-scores obtained if the measurement were off by the measurement variability in either direction (1.6 to 5 mm across studies, ASE/EACVI 2015; default 3 mm). All reference sets are transthoracic echo, end-diastole, leading edge to leading edge (the annulus mid-systole, inner edge). Other conventions trigger a warning describing the direction of the expected bias; values are not converted.

Decision support

Diameter thresholds are summarised from the 2022 ACC/AHA aortic disease guideline and the 2024 ESC guidelines for peripheral arterial and aortic diseases, for the cause selected, using the largest of the sinus, junction and ascending diameters. ACC/AHA indexed thresholds: aortic size index ≥ 3.08 cm/m², aortic height index ≥ 3.21 cm/m and cross-sectional area/height ≥ 10 cm²/m (area = π × (diameter/2)²). The bicuspid phenotype is set automatically to “root” when the sinus diameter is at least the ascending diameter, and can be overridden. Rapid growth found in Follow-up is counted as a risk feature. Not encoded: ACC/AHA thresholds for Loeys–Dietz syndrome (gene-specific), ESC thresholds for heritable disease without an identified gene, Turner syndrome and vascular Ehlers–Danlos syndrome. The panel supports, and does not replace, a multidisciplinary aortic team.

Follow-up

Growth is compared with the latest earlier study done with the same imaging method (echo, or CT/MRI), because differences between methods in one patient can be several millimetres. The 2022 ACC/AHA definition of rapid growth is applied: at least 5 mm in 1 year or at least 3 mm per year across 2 consecutive years for sporadic aneurysms, and at least 3 mm in 1 year with a bicuspid valve or heritable disease. Changes no larger than the measurement variability are flagged. Age at each earlier study is derived from the dates; height and weight are assumed unchanged.

Report import

Pasted text is searched for the four levels (in English or Spanish), age, sex, height, weight, the study date and the imaging method. Values in cm are converted to mm; numbers without a unit below 10 are read as cm. “Aortic root” without further detail is read as the sinuses of Valsalva. Every value is shown for checking before it is applied.

Aortic stiffness

Strain = (systolic − diastolic diameter) / diastolic diameter. Distensibility = 2 × strain / pulse pressure, in 10⁻³ mmHg⁻¹ and 10⁻⁶ cm²·dyn⁻¹ (1 mmHg = 1,333.22 dyn/cm²). Stiffness index β = ln(systolic / diastolic pressure) / strain. When entered, the measured strain drives the pulsation of the patient model; otherwise both models use the mean strain of 15.1 ± 3.6% reported in 405 normotensive adults (mean age 42).

Heartbeat

Weissler AM et al. (Circulation 1968;37:149–59): QS2 = 546 − 2.1 × HR ms in men and 549 − 2.0 × HR ms in women; LVET = 413 − 1.7 × HR ms in men and 418 − 1.6 × HR ms in women, derived at rest. Ventricular motion gives an ejection fraction of about 60% and about 12 mm of mitral annular descent. Atrial motion, the ECG and the shape of the pulsation curve are illustrative.

Views and geometry

The short-axis view is perpendicular to the aortic axis. The parasternal long-axis view is a fixed plane through the root axis, turned to pass close to the left ventricle, left atrium and right ventricular outflow tract, with anterior at the top and the aorta to the right. The sinus segment is 20 mm tall, the sinus diameter is taken at its midpoint and the proximal ascending aorta 15 mm above the junction. Arch, branches, descending aorta, chambers, valves, coronary arteries, pulmonary vessels and veins are stylized. The airway, oesophagus and spine are not modelled.
